## Lead Magnet 1: Prime Readiness Checklist
**Promise:** In 5 minutes, identify the first CMMC readiness gaps a prime is likely to notice.

### Landing page section
**Headline:** Before your prime asks for proof, know what breaks first.

**Subhead:** A plain-English CMMC readiness checklist for GovCon subcontractors who need clarity before a full audit bill.

**Bullets:**
- Check whether your SPRS score story is current enough to survive scrutiny.
- Find the 3 evidence gaps most likely to delay an RFP, renewal, or prime review.
- Compress CMMC uncertainty into 5 workstreams: access, logging, incidents, suppliers, training.
- Decide whether you need a 48-hour gap analysis before spending $5K–$15K on a traditional assessment.

**Form fields:** first name, work email, company, role, current SPRS status dropdown (`known`, `unknown`, `outdated`, `not applicable`).

**CTA button:** Get the checklist

**Post-submit redirect:** thank-you page with checklist + soft audit CTA.

### Checklist content outline
1. **SPRS Snapshot**
   - Do we know the current score?
   - Was it updated in the last 12 months?
   - Can we explain score movement in one paragraph?
2. **Evidence Packet Readiness**
   - SSP location and owner
   - POA&M location and owner
   - policy set location
   - proof screenshots / logs / tickets
3. **5-Workstream Scan**
   - Access control: who can access controlled data?
   - Logging + monitoring: what evidence exists?
   - Incident response: who owns escalation?
   - Supplier governance: which vendors touch sensitive systems?
   - Workforce training: when was training last completed?
4. **Prime Request Simulation**
   - If a prime asked this week, what would take longest to produce?
   - Who needs to approve the response?
   - Which answer depends on one overloaded person?
5. **Next Step Decision Tree**
   - If score unknown/outdated → run gap analysis.
   - If evidence scattered → build packet.
   - If owner unclear → assign 5 workstream owners.
   - If renewal/RFP inside 90 days → prioritize 48-hour audit.
