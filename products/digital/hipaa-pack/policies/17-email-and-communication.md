## 1. Purpose

To set rules for email, texting, phone, fax, and other communications involving PHI at [PRACTICE NAME].

## 2. Email

### 2.1 Practice Email Accounts
- All PHI-related email is sent and received only via practice-managed email ([DOMAIN]), which is encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest.
- Auto-forwarding practice email to personal accounts is prohibited.
- Personal email accounts must not be used for PHI, ever.

### 2.2 Emailing Patients
- Email to patients requires the patient's documented consent ([CONSENT FORM LOCATION]); document their acknowledgment that email is not fully secure.
- Keep emails minimal: [APPROVED CONTENT — e.g., appointment confirmations, generic instructions]. Avoid diagnoses or detailed clinical information unless necessary and consented.
- Verify recipient addresses before sending; use [DOUBLE-CHECK PROCEDURE].
- Misdirected email is an incident — report immediately per HPP-009.

### 2.3 Emailing Providers/Other Entities
- Confirm recipient identity and address; minimum necessary content.
- For attachments containing ePHI, use the secure portal ([PORTAL]) or encrypted attachment ([METHOD]) rather than plain email.

## 3. Text Messaging

- Standard SMS is not secure. Texting PHI is permitted only via [APPROVED SECURE MESSAGING APP] or with patient consent for limited [APPOINTMENT REMINDER] content.
- No clinical photos of patients on personal phones.

## 4. Telephone

- Verify caller identity before discussing PHI (callback to number on file).
- Do not leave detailed PHI on voicemail; leave [APPROVED MINIMAL MESSAGE] only.
- Use headsets and private areas; avoid PHI conversations in hallways and shared spaces.

## 5. Fax

- Use pre-programmed numbers; verify number before sending.
- Use a cover sheet with confidentiality statement.
- Confirm receipt for sensitive transmissions; retrieve faxes promptly; shred misdirected or unwanted faxes.

## 6. Social Media & Public Posting

- No PHI may be posted to social media, review sites, or public forums. Responding to online reviews must never reference a patient or their care without written authorization. Even "confirming" someone is a patient is a violation.

## 7. Marketing Communications

- Communications that constitute marketing require written patient authorization, with limited exceptions (face-to-face communication, refill reminders) — 45 CFR §164.508(a)(3).

## 8. Training

Staff receive communication-specific training at hire and [ANNUALLY]; violations handled per HPP-007.
