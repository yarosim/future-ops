## 1. Purpose

To define how [PRACTICE NAME] determines whether a breach of unsecured PHI occurred and how it notifies affected individuals, HHS, and (when required) the media, per the Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR §164.400–414).

## 2. Key Definitions (§164.402)

- **Breach**: An impermissible acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of unsecured PHI that compromises its security or privacy.
- **Unsecured PHI**: PHI not rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable to unauthorized persons through encryption (NIST-standard) or destruction.
- **Exceptions (not breaches)**: unintentional access by a workforce member acting in good faith within authority and no further disclosure; inadvertent disclosure between authorized persons at the same entity; good-faith belief the unauthorized recipient could not retain the information.

## 3. Breach Determination Process

Every impermissible use or disclosure is **presumed** a breach unless a documented four-factor risk assessment demonstrates a low probability of compromise:

1. **Nature and extent of the PHI**, including identifiers and likelihood of re-identification.
2. **The unauthorized person** who used or received the PHI.
3. **Whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed.**
4. **The extent to which risk has been mitigated.**

The Privacy Officer completes the assessment within [5 BUSINESS DAYS] of discovery and documents it in the Incident Log regardless of outcome.

## 4. Notification Requirements

### 4.1 Affected Individuals (§164.404)
- **Timing**: Without unreasonable delay, no later than 60 calendar days after discovery.
- **Method**: Written notice by first-class mail (or email if individual agreed). Substitute notice (website posting for 90 days or major media) if 10+ addresses are invalid.
- **Content**: Brief description of what happened; date and date of discovery; types of PHI involved; steps individuals should take; what the Practice is doing; contact procedures including a toll-free number.

### 4.2 HHS Secretary (§164.408)
- **500+ individuals**: Contemporaneous with individual notice, via the HHS breach portal.
- **Under 500**: Within 60 days after the end of the calendar year of discovery, via the HHS breach portal.

### 4.3 Media (§164.406)
- If 500+ residents of a state or jurisdiction are affected, notice to prominent media outlets serving that area within 60 days.

### 4.4 Business Associates (§164.410)
Business associates must notify the Practice without unreasonable delay, no later than 60 days from discovery, and provide the information needed for the Practice's own notifications.

## 5. Encryption as Safe Harbor

ePHI encrypted to NIST standards (e.g., AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.2+ in transit) with keys uncompromised is "secured" — its loss is generally not a reportable breach. Document the encryption status of all devices in [ASSET INVENTORY LOCATION].

## 6. Delay for Law Enforcement

Notification may be delayed if a law enforcement official determines notification would impede a criminal investigation (§164.412). Document the request in writing.

## 7. Records

All breach determinations, assessments, notices, and HHS submissions are retained for at least [6] years (§164.414).
