## 1. Purpose

To define how [PRACTICE NAME] limits uses, disclosures, and requests of protected health information (PHI) to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose, as required by 45 CFR §164.502(b) and §164.514(d).

## 2. Scope

Applies to all workforce members and business associates of the Practice, and to all PHI in any form, except as excluded in Section 5.

## 3. General Rule

For uses, disclosures, and requests of PHI — other than those in Section 5 — workforce members must limit the PHI used, disclosed, or requested to:

- The least amount of information reasonably necessary; and
- The fewest people reasonably necessary to accomplish the purpose.

## 4. Implementation

### 4.1 Role-Based Access
Access to PHI is granted by role. The Privacy Officer maintains the access matrix below and reviews it [ANNUALLY].

| Role | Access Level |
|---|---|
| [Physician/Provider] | Full medical record for patients under care |
| [Nurse/MA] | Clinical record for assigned patients |
| [Front Desk/Reception] | Demographics, scheduling, insurance — not clinical notes |
| [Billing Staff] | Demographics, insurance, charges, coding data |
| [Practice Manager] | Administrative and business data |
| [IT Support] | System-level access, logged; no routine viewing of clinical content |

### 4.2 Disclosures
- Respond to record requests with only the records specifically identified. If a request is overly broad, contact the requester to narrow it before producing.
- Verbal disclosures should occur in private settings away from waiting areas.

### 4.3 Requests for PHI
When requesting PHI from another provider, limit requests to information needed for the specific purpose (e.g., request cardiology notes only, not the entire chart).

### 4.4 Internal Review
The Privacy Officer audits [MONTHLY/QUARTERLY] a sample of access logs and disclosures to confirm compliance with minimum necessary.

## 5. Exceptions

Minimum necessary does **not** apply to (45 CFR §164.502(b)(2)):

1. Disclosures to or requests by a health care provider for treatment.
2. Uses or disclosures made to the individual (the patient).
3. Uses or disclosures made pursuant to a patient's written authorization.
4. Disclosures to HHS for compliance investigation or review.
5. Uses or disclosures required by law.
6. Disclosures of the facility directory to persons identified in §164.510(c), or de-identified information (§164.514(a)–(b)).
